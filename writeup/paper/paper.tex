\title{Searching for six coprime representations as a sum of two cubes}
\author{David Curran}
\date{\today}
\address{University College Dublin, Ireland} 
\email{david.curran1@ucd.ie}
\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
The sequence of least positive integers with $k$ representations as $x^3 + y^3$ with $\gcd(x, y) = 1$, where $x$
and $y$ may be negative, is known up to the fifth term. This number $506433677359393$ was found by Bernstein in 1999, and the sixth is unknown. It is sequence A047697 in the OEIS, and we report two searches for its sixth term. The first search is exhaustive: it uses GPUs to search every $n \le 2.5\cdot10^{23}$. The search proves
A047697(6) $> 2.5\cdot10^{23}$, a millionfold beyond Bernstein's bound, and finds exactly twelve coprime five-ways in
that range. Since a cubefree number has only coprime representations, it also gives a lower bound for the cubefree taxicab numbers (OEIS A080642):
A080642(5) $> 2.5\cdot10^{23}$. The second search uses the geometry of the elliptic curve $x^3 + y^3 = n$: in every
example we tested ($1{,}378$ numbers), the integer points are related only by lines (three collinear points) and hubs
(two chords meeting at the same rational point), so we enumerate lines and hubs directly. It is not exhaustive but
reaches far larger $n$, and it has found 70 further coprime five-ways, up to $3.8\cdot10^{35}$. We prove that no line
carries three points with positive coordinates, so a cubefree number with many positive representations must come
from hubs or high rank; a search restricted to positive hubs finds eight cubefree numbers with four positive
representations. We found no six-way, and no cubefree five-way. We release the catalogue of $8{,}928$ coprime
four-ways and 82 five-ways, complete for $n \le 2.5\cdot10^{23}$, together with the
code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\begin{points}{the problem}
\item Hardy and Ramanujan, 1729; the taxicab numbers Ta$(k)$ (OEIS A011541) and cabtaxi numbers Ca$(k)$ (A047696, negative cubes allowed).
\item Ta$(k)$ exists for every $k$ (Hardy and Wright, Theorem 412), but only by scaling rational points by cubes; the numbers it gives are far from coprime.
\item The coprime version A047697 and the cubefree version A080642 forbid scaling, so a $k$-way needs $k$ genuinely distinct integer points. Whether they exist for every $k$ is open; Silverman \cite{silverman1993} links it to the rank of $x^3+y^3=n$ being unbounded; Stewart \cite{stewart2008,stewart2009} on cubic Thue equations with many solutions.
\item Known terms: A047697 = 1, 91, 3367, 16776487 (Rathbun), 506433677359393 (Bernstein); A080642 = 2, 1729, 15170835645, 1801049058342701083 (Hollerbach's list).
\item Relatives in the OEIS: A023050, A293645--A293651 (Fay, 2017).
\end{points}
\begin{points}{best current results}
\item Coprime: Bernstein \cite{bernstein2001} found A047697(5) and searched to $2.5\cdot10^{17}$; no later search known.
\item Cubefree: A080642(4) from Hollerbach's search; no published bound for A080642(5).
\item Taxicab: Wilson \cite{wilson1999} Ta(5); Calude, Calude and Dinneen \cite{caludedinneen2003} Ta(6) with high probability; Hollerbach \cite{hollerbach2008} announced an exhaustive confirmation; Eisermann \cite{eisermann2008} Ta(6) $>5\cdot10^{20}$.
\item Upper bounds for Ta$(k)$ and Ca$(k)$: Boyer \cite{boyer2008}, Su \cite{su2016} (context only; our own bounds are a separate paper).
\item High rank in this family: Elkies and Rogers \cite{elkiesrogers2004}.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{websites}
\item OEIS entries \cite{oeisA047697,oeisA080642}; Boyer's taxicab pages (current bound tables); Butler's pages \cite{butler} (primitive lists complete to $10^{23}$). Cite papers for results, websites for data.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{two families of algorithm}
\item Exhaustive enumeration: list every $a^3\pm b^3\le B$ and find repeats; all methods cost about $B^{2/3}$ (the number of pairs) and differ in how repeats are found: a heap in increasing order (Wilson, Bernstein; memory $B^{1/3}$), bimonotone enumeration (Eisermann), bands plus a hash table (Butler).
\item Construction from rational points: take $x^3+y^3=M$ with many rational points and clear denominators with a cube (Boyer, Su, Butler above $10^{23}$). Gives the Ta/Ca upper bounds, but never a coprime or cubefree number, since the scaling factor divides every representation.
\item So the coprime problem needs brute force or a different structure: this is the motivation for our two searches.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{what this paper does}
\item Exhaustive GPU search to $2.5\cdot10^{23}$: A047697(6) $>2.5\cdot10^{23}$, hence A080642(5) $>2.5\cdot10^{23}$; exactly 12 five-ways in that range.
\item The geometry: only lines and hubs relate the integer points; theorems that make lines searchable; convexity (no line holds three positive points).
\item A targeted search by lines and hubs: 70 further five-ways up to $3.8\cdot10^{35}$; eight all-positive cubefree four-ways; no six-way.
\item A complete line search: every $n\le10^{30}$ with a line in a direction of cost $\le1.2\cdot10^{12}$ counted exactly (1.6 million directions, $1.8\cdot10^{11}$ slopes, rigorous per-direction heights): no six-way; the class holds 97\% of the known five-ways below $10^{30}$. Repeated in C++ to $n\le\lfloor 10^{33}\rfloor_{\rm float}$ (1.3$\cdot10^{12}$ slopes): no six-way, 131 five-ways, 49 new. Height bounds certified exactly (Lemma 15, Sturm sequences).
\item Catalogue of 8,928 four-ways and 82 five-ways, complete to $2.5\cdot10^{23}$; OEIS extensions (A023050 to $10^{19}$, A293648, A293649, A293651 with 14 missing terms).
\item Outline of the sections.
\end{points}

\section{The exhaustive search}
\begin{points}{what is counted}
\item Every coprime representation of $n\le B$, written once: sums $a^3+b^3$ with $1\le a\le b$, and differences $(c+d)^3-c^3 = d^3+3dc(c+d)$ with $c,d\ge1$.
\item About $1.33\,B^{2/3}$ pairs, sums about a third of them; $6/\pi^2\approx 60.8\%$ coprime (Baker \cite{baker2007}, Liu \cite{liu2015} for the count).
\end{points}
\begin{points}{the algorithm}
\item Same family as Butler's: cut $(0,B]$ into segments, generate every pair whose value falls in a segment, find repeats inside the segment. Butler hashes; we sort on a GPU.
\item Segments equal in $n^{2/3}$ (equal numbers of pairs); a segment that is too large is split; segments are independent, so $k$ GPUs take equal shares of the work.
\item On the GPU, per segment: count (one thread per stream, exact cube roots or an exact quadratic bound), prefix-sum, write (additions only; values stored as 64-bit offsets $n-\mathrm{lo}$, so 128-bit values sort as 64-bit keys; binary gcd removes non-coprime pairs), radix sort, mark runs of length $\ge k_{\min}$.
\item On the CPU, every reported $n$ re-derived independently in exact 128-bit arithmetic; the count must equal the GPU's multiplicity.
\item Number theory only for checking, never for pruning (THEOREMS 5; the divisor test, cf.\ Broughan \cite{broughan2003}), so ``no six-way up to $B$'' remains a proof.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{validation}
\item Brute force to $10^7$; known terms; splitting a range gives the same pairs; a window above $2^{64}$; agreement with the CPU program at $10^{12}$ and $10^{15}$; self-test on every GPU before every run; every $k\ge4$ number recounted from its factorisation; 0--$10^{22}$ run twice with identical output; the CPU program's 205 numbers with $k\ge4$ in $10^{17}$--$10^{19}$ equal the GPU's; the GPU fill of $10^{22}$--$10^{23}$ re-found all 275 four-ways the targeted searches had found there.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{runs, cost and results}
\item Table of ranges, hardware, pairs and time (about 140 GPU-hours on RTX 5090s, plus 7of9's RTX 2080 Ti runs); sort is 60\% of the time.
\item Results: A047697(6) $>2.5\cdot10^{23}$; the 12 five-ways by decade (none in $10^{22}$--$10^{23}$); complete four-way lists.
\item Comparison: Bernstein $2.5\cdot10^{17}$; Eisermann's taxicab enumeration to $5\cdot10^{20}$ (about 650 CPU-days); Butler complete to $10^{23}$ (positive, primitive).
\item Limits and next steps: the $10^{24}$ guard; bucket counting instead of a full sort (Butler's hash idea on the GPU), about $2\times$.
\end{points}

\section{The geometry of \texorpdfstring{$x^3 + y^3 = n$}{x^3 + y^3 = n}}
\begin{points}{the curve}
\item $C_n: x^3+y^3=n$ is an elliptic curve; its point at infinity $O=(1:-1:0)$ is a flex. With $O$ as zero: three points are collinear exactly when $P+Q+R=0$, and $-(x,y)=(y,x)$.
\item A representation of $n$ gives the points $(x,y)$ and $(y,x)$; a line through three integer points is a three-term relation.
\item The third point of a chord: the classical formula of Werebrusow and Desboves (Dickson \cite{dickson1920}, ch.~XXI); example on 3367. The mechanism is classical; the census and the search are new.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{the shapes of a multi-way}
\item Line + free point; bowtie (two lines through a shared point, five points); hub ($P_1+P_2=P_3+P_4$: two chords meeting at the same non-integral point $R$ of small denominator $D$; a hub of $n$ is a bowtie of $D^3n$); independent.
\item Table: 204 four-ways $\le10^{17}$: 184 line + free, 11 hub, 9 independent; 214 four-ways in $10^{23}$--$2.5\cdot10^{23}$: 188 / 24 / 2.
\item Figure 1: the curve with a bowtie and a hub drawn on it.
\item [Draft point by Claude, 10 Nov 2026 -- to rewrite.] One genus-1 curve gives both shapes. The rational points of a bowtie curve (fixed base and second direction; FINDINGS F53, F86) are configurations of a base line and a second line through the shared point; the base triple is always integral after scaling, and the second line contributes 2, 1 or 0 coprime integral points. Two gives a bowtie (five-way), one gives line + free point (four-way: the free point's chord to the shared point has a non-integral third point). On $(3,4)+(9,52)$, 3,124 points: 800 bowties, 343 line + free candidates (251 distinct $n$), 384 base line only; all 19 candidates counted exactly (23--120 digits) are four-ways with one line (FINDINGS F87).
\end{points}

\begin{points}{only lines and hubs (an observation, not a theorem)}
\item For 1,378 coprime numbers with $k\ge4$: height-pairing matrix of the integral points, integer relations by LLL (coefficients $\le1000$), each checked exactly with the group law.
\item All 1,403 relations found have 3 or 4 terms with every coefficient $\pm1$: lines and hubs. No tangents, no longer relations.
\item State the limits: coefficients up to 1,000, this sample of numbers.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{structure against rank}
\item Independent multi-ways need rank $\ge4$; their curves have mean rank 5.3 (exact ranks 4--8), against 3.8 for line + free and 4.2 for hubs.
\item Two mechanisms: structure (relations; the points span rank 3) and rank (no relations, unusually high rank). A080642(4) is a pure rank example (rank 4).
\end{points}

\section{Lines that can carry three coprime points}
\begin{points}{notation}
\item Line $ax+by=c$, $\gcd(a,b)=1$; cost $D=b^3-a^3$; $E=a^2+ab+b^2$, so $D=(b-a)E$. The cubic in $x$ after substituting the line; Vieta.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{results to state and prove in full (proofs in \texttt{experiments/mining/THEOREMS.md})}
\item Theorem 1 (direction rule): three distinct coprime points on a line force $ab>0$, $a\ne b$, $ab$ a norm from $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, and $4\mid ab$. Proof via the conic $X^2+XY+Y^2=3ab^3$ and a 2-adic argument. Part (a) probably implicit in Werebrusow; part (b) not found in the literature. Check: all 11,155 lines up to $10^{17}$ obey it.
\item Theorem 2: $E$ divides $c$ and $n$.
\item Theorem 4 (complete enumeration): the coprime collinear triples in a direction are the rational points of the conic, i.e.\ the reduced slopes through one point $P_0$; so ``searched to height $H$'' is exact.
\item Theorem 7 (D-lines): the denominator $D$ of the third point divides $(b-a)E$; even $D$ forces $a,b$ odd. Open converse, as a conjecture: $a,b$ both odd forces $D$ even.
\item Theorem 8 (types at 2 and 3): for $n$ odd, exactly one coordinate of each point is even; for $n\equiv\pm1 \pmod 9$, exactly one is divisible by 3; points on a line share a type. Corollary: no complete quadrilateral of six coprime representations when $n$ is odd or $n\equiv\pm1\pmod 9$.
\item Theorem 13 (the $s$-cubic): for three collinear points, $s_i=x_i+y_i$ are the roots of $Ez^3-3c(a+b)z^2+3c^2z-(a-b)^2n$. Corollary: for $p\not\equiv1\pmod 3$, $v_p(b-a)=v_p(s)$, so the direction fixes the non-split part of $n$; $p=2$ gives Theorem 12. Found by mining, checked on 492,028 lines.
\item Theorem 14: for every chord through two coprime points, the non-split part of $n$ divides $b-a$ (lines: equality); the denominator of $x_R+y_R$ divides $E$; the two chords of a hub satisfy $(a-b)^2E's_3s_4=(a'-b')^2Es_1s_2$. Checked on 3.16 million pairs of points.
\item Theorem 12 (even $n$ has no lines): for even $n$ all points are odd, and with Theorem 1(b) the sum of roots in Vieta has the wrong parity. Explains why even $n$ are 33\% of sums but 0.7\% of four-ways (all hubs) and no five-way. Found by mining.
\item Theorem 10 (primitive third point): the chord through two coprime integer points meets the curve again at $R=(X/D,Y/D)$ with $\gcd(X,Y)=1$, so every hub point is a primitive point of $D^3n$. Short proof by Vieta at a prime $p\mid X,Y$. Found by mining the hubs, then proved; fails for non-coprime points.
\item Theorem 9: $D\mid X+Y$ iff $D\mid b-a$ (the coordinate sum of $R$ is an integer exactly when $D\mid b-a$); with Theorem 7, $D$ splits into a part dividing $b-a$ and a part from $E$.
\item Proposition (convexity): no line meets $x^3+y^3=n$ in three points with positive coordinates. Short proof; consequence for \S7.
\item [Draft point by Claude, 10 Nov 2026 -- to rewrite.] Theorem 21 (line patterns are decided by linear algebra): if $n$ has a line it is odd (Theorem 12), so every coprime representation has a 2-type; orient each representation to type $+1$, giving a point $q_i$. By Theorem 8 every line becomes $q_a+q_b+q_c=0$ in the torsion-free group of the curve. So a pattern of lines among $k$ representations is impossible whenever its relations force $q_i=0$, $q_i=q_j$ or $q_i=-q_j$ -- a finite linear-algebra test. Theorem 8's complete quadrilateral is the one-line special case ($A+B+C=A+D+E=0$ and $B+D+X=C+E+X=0$ give $2A=2X$). Also impossible: the Fano plane (7 points), the M\"obius--Kantor configuration (8), Pappus (9), Hesse (9 points, 12 lines) and Desargues (10) -- for example, no 10-way has its representations in the Desargues configuration. M\"obius--Kantor, Pappus and Hesse contain no complete quadrilateral, so this is strictly stronger than Theorem 8. Exhaustive search: $k$ coprime representations carry at most $1,1,2,3,4,6$ lines for $k=3,\dots,8$ (against $1,1,2,4,7,8$ for points in general). (THEOREMS.md Theorem 21; FINDINGS F90.)
\end{points}
\begin{points}{supporting lemma, with a citation}
\item Theorem 5: primes $\not\equiv1 \pmod 3$ in a coprime representation (via $n=st$, $s=x+y$, $t=x^2-xy+y^2$); largely classical.
\end{points}

\section{The curve search}
\begin{points}{the idea}
\item Most multi-ways are tied together by lines (three collinear integer points) and hubs; so instead of enumerating pairs, enumerate lines and hubs, and count every number they produce exactly.
\item Not exhaustive: it finds numbers, it cannot rule them out. Reaches $n$ far beyond the GPU search.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{lines}
\item Directions $(a,b)$ of lines $ax+by=c$ that can carry three coprime points: Theorem 1 ($ab>0$, a norm from $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, $4\mid ab$); cost $b^3-a^3$; all allowed directions in increasing cost.
\item For each direction, the collinear triples are the rational points of the conic $X^2+XY+Y^2=3ab^3$ (Theorem 4): sweep the slopes $r/s$ through one point found by \texttt{qfsolve}, up to a height; integer arithmetic throughout.
\item Height per direction from $n\approx \mathrm{cost}^2h^6$, stopping where $n$ passes the cap (keeps 94\% of candidates in 36\% of the time).
\end{points}
\begin{points}{filter and exact counting}
\item Keep $n$ with at least 7 split primes ($p\equiv1 \bmod 3$) below 1,000 and two of $\{7,13,19,37\}$; why (five-ways are rich in split primes; the free-point rate doubles per extra prime).
\item Count every kept $n$ exactly from its factorisation: for each divisor $s\le(4n)^{1/3}$ test whether $(4n/s-s^2)/3$ is a square (about $10^4$ numbers per second per core). Any extra representation is found whatever its shape.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{D-lines and hubs}
\item A D-line: two integer points and a third of denominator $D$; scaled by $D$ it is an ordinary line on $D^3n$ (Theorem 7); two D-lines through the same point form a hub.
\item Only one chord of a hub has to be enumerated; exact counting finds the other.
\item Positive hubs: the slopes whose line has two positive points form three arcs; enumerating only those is about $5\times$ cheaper and gives the all-positive hubs needed for the cubefree problem.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{runs and saturation}
\item Machines and runs (Mac, 7of9, worf, rented GPUs for the first line runs); candidates counted.
\item Saturation below $10^{30}$: every direction of cost $\le1.2\cdot10^{12}$ searched; most new five-way finds became rediscoveries.
\item Recall against the exhaustive data (50--70\% of four-ways below $10^{16}$, falling with $n$; higher for five-ways).
\end{points}

\section{Cubefree taxicab numbers}
\begin{points}{the link}
\item A cubefree $n$ has only coprime representations, so a cubefree five-way is a coprime five-way with all representations positive.
\item Bound: A080642(5) $>2.5\cdot10^{23}$ from the exhaustive search; none of the 12 five-ways has five positive representations (the best, 2724248230569896697541, has four). Butler's 357 primitive five-ways and 24 six-ways to $2.5\cdot10^{28}$: none coprime, so none cubefree.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{why it is hard}
\item Convexity: no line holds three positive points, so the line search can never produce an all-positive multi-way; only hubs or rank can.
\item Near misses: two coprime five-ways with four positive representations (2724248230569896697541 and 133258206935285167925449341991); in both the difference is the point the two lines share.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{the positive hub search}
\item The slopes whose line has two positive points form three arcs; enumerating only those (validated against the full sweep) is about $5\times$ cheaper.
\item Result: eight all-positive coprime four-ways known, three new and all cubefree hubs (table: $n$, factorisation, representations, meeting-point denominator).
\item All-positive hubs are rarer than independent chords would predict (0 of 199, then 3 of 56); positive hubs meet at larger denominators.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{routes that do not work}
\item Elkies--Rogers record curves: 0 or 1 integer points each.
\item Cost estimates: exhaustive to the likely size (near $10^{27}$ by a rough extrapolation) is out of reach; hubs need hundreds of nights; random rich $n$ almost never have any representation.
\end{points}

\section{Why no six-way?}
\begin{points}{the shapes a six-way could take}
\item Gap principle (Helfgott--Venkatesh \cite{helfgottvenkatesh2006}): the integral points of a multi-way lie on a thin height shell at mutual angles $\ge60^\circ$ (data: median spread 4.5\%, $|\cos|\le\frac12$ in all 2,832 pairs).
\item Six such points in rank 3 must form the complete quadrilateral, excluded by Theorem 8; what remains needs rank $\ge4$: bowtie + free point, two disjoint lines, line + hub, or rank alone.
\end{points}

\begin{points}{how rare}
\item The free-point rate per line candidate, rising with split primes and falling with $n$; predicted one six-way per 300--1,500 five-ways; expected number in our searches about 0.02--0.08, so finding none is no surprise.
\item Rates on the class-complete set to $10^{30}$ (8,898 four-ways, 64 five-ways): five : four steady at about 1:140; bowtie $7\cdot10^{-3}$ per line + free four-way; extra free point $3\cdot10^{-4}$, about 3$\times$ lower than earlier estimates, so six : five about 1:1,500 (range 1:500--1:5,000) and about 0.1 six-ways expected below $10^{30}$.
\item A ladder (table): how many $(k-1)$-ways come before the first $k$-way. Before 91: 11 coprime sums (12 counting $1=1^3+0^3$); before 3367: 5 two-ways; before 16776487: 24 three-ways; before 506433677359393: 80 four-ways; below $2.5\cdot10^{23}$: 12 five-ways and no six-way. From $k=3$ on the step grows about $3$--$5\times$, which points to a few hundred five-ways before the first six-way, consistent with the 1 : 300--1,500 estimate. Each rung is a single sample, so treat it as an order of magnitude.
\item Where might A047697(6) be? The gaps between known terms in decades are 1.6, 3.7, 7.5 (roughly doubling), suggesting near $10^{30}$; compare with the extrapolation of Figure 2. Hedge clearly.
\end{points}
\begin{points}{negative results}
\item Divisor-richness alone does not make multi-ways: of 20,000 random products of 12 split primes, none has three representations (structure first, divisors second).
\item Polynomial families do not help (N4).
\end{points}
\begin{points}{open problems}

\item Are lines and hubs the only relations among the integer points?
\item The converse half of Theorem 7.
\item Why all-positive hubs are rarer than expected.
\end{points}
